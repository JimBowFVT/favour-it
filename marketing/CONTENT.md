Owner: Adam Levi. Twelve draft posts, three per week. Planning dates are in the workbook. Review and publish manually. No post has been published.

Use plain text, a relevant work example or a short screen recording when available. No fabricated customers, traction, savings, testimonials or promised earnings. Add a public application link only when the page is approved for public use. Personalise the language so it sounds like you.

## Week 1 — explain the problem

### Post 01 — Why I'm building Favourit

Creative people need other creative people.

A designer may need a video edit for their portfolio. An editor may need better copy for their website. Being good at your own craft doesn't mean you have the time or budget to do everything else.

I'm building Favourit to explore a different way to get that help: offer a small service, earn FAV, and use FAV on another member's service.

We're still in development. The first pilot will have no cash-out, and joining won't guarantee work or matches.

I'm starting by speaking with designers, editors and writers about the last time they needed help on a project of their own.

What task have you been putting off because it needs someone else's skill?

### Post 02 — Scope makes collaboration easier

“I'll help with your branding” leaves a lot to interpret.

“I'll review up to five portfolio pages and send three prioritised improvements” gives both people something concrete to agree on.

A useful service package answers five questions:

- What will I receive?
- What do I need to provide?
- When will it arrive?
- How many revisions are included?
- What is outside the scope?

Before choosing a price, write those answers. It's a small exercise that can prevent a much larger misunderstanding.

Which part of scoping a project causes you the most friction?

### Post 03 — What the pilot needs to prove

For Favourit's first pilot, I want to learn something specific: can a small group of creatives get useful help by contributing their own skills?

That means finding people with both an offer and a need. A long list of profiles won't answer the question.

I'm focusing on design, short-form video and writing, and keeping the first group small enough to support personally.

FAV will be usable inside the community. Cash-out won't be available during the pilot, so this isn't a promise of cash income.

If you work in one of those areas and have a project you need help with, I'd value your perspective.

## Week 2 — make the exchange understandable

### Post 04 — An example, not a result

Here is an illustrative Favourit journey:

A designer reviews someone's portfolio. After completing the order, they earn FAV. Later, they use available FAV towards a video edit for their own portfolio.

The video editor doesn't have to be the person who bought the review. That is the purpose of using a shared unit instead of requiring a direct swap.

This is an example of the proposed experience, not a customer story. Suitable services need to be available, and the balance needs to cover the order and buyer fee.

The pilot has no cash-out and no guaranteed matches.

What would you need to see before feeling comfortable trying this?

### Post 05 — A better buyer brief

A useful brief doesn't have to be long.

For a 30-second edit, I would want to know:

- The audience and intended result.
- Where the video will be posted.
- Which footage and assets are available.
- Any examples of the desired style.
- The deadline and approval process.

“Make it engaging” means different things to different people. A concrete example gives a collaborator a much better starting point.

What is the one question you always ask before starting creative work?

### Post 06 — An honest limit

One thing I want to make clear about Favourit from the beginning: cash-out will not be available in the first services-for-FAV pilot.

The question we're testing is whether people value the services they can obtain inside a small creative community.

For some freelancers, cash income is the priority. That is a valid reason this pilot may not fit.

I'd rather hear that early than persuade someone to contribute work under the wrong expectation.

If you've tried a service exchange before, what made it useful—or made you stop?

## Week 3 — show the support and standards

### Post 07 — Your first service can be small

A first service doesn't need to be an entire brand, website or campaign.

It might be a focused portfolio review, a short edit from supplied footage, or a rewrite of a homepage introduction.

The goal is a useful, clearly bounded result that both people understand.

For Favourit's founding group, I'm planning to help members shape one service and identify one service they need. Members set their own scope and price.

What small deliverable do people already ask you for?

### Post 08 — A closer look at the fees

Here is a simple illustration of Favourit's current marketplace fee model.

For a service priced at 100 FAV:

- The buyer pays 103 FAV, including a 3 FAV buyer fee.
- The seller receives 97 FAV after a 3 FAV seller fee.

These are FAV amounts, not a cash exchange rate. Actual order amounts are confirmed at checkout. Cash-out is unavailable during the pilot.

Fees should be visible before either person commits. The service scope should be just as clear.

What information do you look for before trusting a new marketplace?

### Post 09 — How I'd like to build this

I want Favourit's early development to include the people who might use it.

That means asking about a recent real problem, listening when the idea doesn't fit, and showing what changes as a result.

The first group will be small. I plan to help with onboarding and introductions, while being clear that I can't guarantee orders or matches.

If you could improve one part of finding help for your own creative projects, what would you change first?

## Week 4 — invite qualified participation

### Post 10 — What belongs in a work sample

When showing a work sample, help the reader understand the work.

What was the brief? What did you personally contribute? What constraints mattered? What changed between the starting point and the final deliverable?

A clear explanation can make a small project useful evidence of your skills.

If several people worked on it, credit their contributions. Only share material you have permission to show.

What do you wish clients noticed in your portfolio?

### Post 11 — A complementary community

I'm interested in a creative community where members can explain both what they offer and what they need.

“I design thumbnails, and I need help editing an introduction video” tells us much more than a job title alone.

For Favourit, that information will guide whom we invite and which gaps we try to fill.

The first pilot uses FAV for services inside the community, with no cash-out or guaranteed matches.

Finish these two sentences if you'd like: I can help with ____. Right now, I need help with ____.
