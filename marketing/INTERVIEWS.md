# Research and comprehension guide

## Invitation and consent

Run a 20-minute conversation. Say: "I'm researching how independent creatives get help with their own projects. I'm not asking you to buy anything. May I take private notes?" Ask separately before recording. Do not record by default.

Store only useful notes and the contact details the person agreed to share. An interview is not permission to add someone to a newsletter.

## Interview script

**Minutes 0–3: context**

- What do you create, and for whom?
- What project of your own are you working on now?

**Minutes 3–10: a real recent need**

- Tell me about the last time you needed someone else's creative skill.
- What exactly needed to be delivered, and by when?
- What did you do? Did you hire someone, ask a friend, do it yourself, or postpone it?
- What did that cost in time or money? A range is fine.
- What was frustrating about that experience?
- What would have made you trust someone to do the work?

Do not pitch yet. Ask for concrete past behaviour rather than whether they like the idea.

**Minutes 10–15: offer and reaction**

Explain: "Favourit is preparing a small pilot. You can provide a scoped service, earn FAV, and spend FAV on another member's service. Cash-out will not be available during this pilot. Orders and matches are not guaranteed."

- In your own words, what would you receive for your work?
- What, if anything, would you want to use FAV for in the next 30 days?
- What small, useful service would you be comfortable offering?
- What would stop you from trying it?
- Would this be useful without cash withdrawal? Why or why not?

Do not argue with a preference for cash. Record it as a business-model signal.

**Minutes 15–20: concrete commitment**

- Would you be willing to prepare that service for a pilot conversation?
- Can you share an existing work sample?
- When could you participate?
- May Adam follow up about this specific research or application?

An informed expression of willingness requires a specific offer, a specific need, understanding that cash-out is unavailable, and explicit willingness to take a next step. Polite praise and waitlist clicks alone do not qualify.

## Five-person page comprehension test

Recruit five target creatives who have not helped write the page. Do not explain the product first.

1. Show the hero briefly, then ask what the site does and whom it serves.
2. Let them browse. Ask how a member earns FAV and what they can do with it.
3. Ask whether they can withdraw money in this pilot.
4. Ask them to find a relevant example and explain what is included and excluded.
5. Ask what they would do next and what happens after applying.

Record each answer as correct, incorrect or unclear, plus the observed confusion. A participant passes comprehension only if they correctly explain the offer, FAV use and no cash-out. The navigation task passes when they find a suitable example and understand the next action without coaching.

Targets: at least four of five pass comprehension and four of five pass navigation. These are internal release checks, not statistically conclusive conversion estimates. If a test fails, fix the repeated confusion and retest with new participants. No results have been collected yet.
