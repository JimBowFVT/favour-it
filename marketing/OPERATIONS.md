# Founding-creators operations

## Screen for useful participation

Use the private application inbox in the existing administrator area. Check a credible work sample, one bounded offer, one concrete need within 30 days, adult eligibility, understanding of no cash-out and permission to contact.

Do not rank by follower count or willingness to write a positive review. Recruit scarce skills that fit logged requests. Limit any one service area to half of the first 30-person cohort. If demand cannot be matched, keep the application waitlisted and explain why.

Application statuses are workflow notes: applied, interviewing, shortlisted, waitlisted, invited, declined, withdrawn. Changing one does not change the app account, send an email or make a financial commitment.

## Weekly matching session

1. Review actual requests, including deadline, scope and available budget in FAV.
2. Identify an available member with relevant proof of work.
3. Ask both members whether they want an introduction.
4. Let the participants agree scope, price, buyer total, turnaround and revisions.
5. Once trading readiness is verified, have the buyer submit the brief and fund the order in Favourit.
6. Log an accepted match only when both participants agree to the relevant service, not when the founder merely suggests it.
7. Follow up on delivery, approval, feedback and the member's next useful purchase.

Do not bypass the order flow by editing wallets or awarding FAV manually. Do not guarantee timing, quality or cash value. If an order becomes disputed, use the existing authorised mediation process.

## Onboarding call: 20 minutes

- Confirm the member understands FAV, fees, the unavailable cash-out and the limits of the pilot.
- Review the service deliverable, exclusions, buyer inputs, turnaround and revisions.
- Confirm the requested service and affordability under existing balances/reward policy.
- Walk through the order, messaging and support paths only when verified ready.
- Ask what next step would make the first week worthwhile.

## Opportunity digest

Send at most once per week, only when useful real requests exist. Check current digest consent and suppress withdrawn/unsubscribed recipients before every send. An application contact permission is not newsletter permission.

Obtain permission before including identifiable member requests or portfolio material. Keep the first cohort's email addresses private; use individual messages or a properly configured opt-in email service, never a public To/Cc list.

Replies requesting unsubscribe or no further contact must be handled before the next send. Mark the application withdrawn when all contact consent is withdrawn, suppress it in the recruitment tracker, and process deletion requests through the owner.

Review research-only applications older than 90 days weekly. Delete those with no continuing agreed purpose. Keep exports private. Store consent dates/version and any withdrawal instructions. Do not put applicant files or real metrics exports into Git or a public website. The delivered blank workbook contains no real applicants.

## Weekly management review

Use one hour each week:

- 10 minutes: inspect sources, denominators and freshness of results.
- 15 minutes: review where real transactions stopped or needed help.
- 15 minutes: read interview and departure evidence.
- 10 minutes: identify the most useful missing skill or product fix.
- 10 minutes: assign next actions and confirm any gate decisions.

Record owner, action, due date and evidence required. Keep budget changes separate from observed performance.

## Case-study permission

Ask separately: "May Favourit publish [specific name/work/quote] in [specified channels]? Here is the exact draft and image. You can decline without affecting your participation."

Record approval and permitted use. Do not request a positive review in exchange for benefits. Do not edit a quote to imply cash earnings, stronger satisfaction or a guarantee. Treat founder-assisted work as assisted when describing results.

## Escalation and stopping rules

- Confusion about cash-out: pause the conversation, explain it plainly, and do not recruit on a mistaken understanding.
- No relevant service available: waitlist or recruit the missing skill; do not invent availability.
- Scope disagreement: get agreement before an order; use the dispute process for existing orders.
- Repeated poor matches or support overload: stop expanding until the cause is understood.
- Cash needed by most qualified interviewees: return to the payment proposition and keep the project in research.
